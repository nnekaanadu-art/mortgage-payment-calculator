How incentives are handled

Ontario and Toronto land transfer taxes and qualifying first-time buyer refunds are estimated automatically. British Columbia property transfer tax and first-time buyer exemption are also estimated. Other Canadian locations and U.S. locations can use a manually entered incentive estimate, because programs vary by jurisdiction, lender, income, property and funding availability.

New-build GST/HST rebates: Potentially significant Canadian rebates exist, including a federal first-time buyer rebate. They are not automatically deducted, because eligibility, purchase agreement dates, tax-included pricing, builder credits and provincial rules must be verified. Use the manual incentive field for an externally verified amount; avoid double counting. U.S. jurisdictions also have first-time buyer assistance and tax or fee relief; these are not Canada-only.

A rebate or grant is treated as an upfront closing-cost offset, not a reduction in the mortgage principal. An actual down-payment assistance loan or grant may be treated differently. Estimates exclude most closing fees, ongoing property tax, homeowners insurance and HOA/condo fees. Canadian default-insurance estimates use indicative premiums and omit provincial sales tax on premiums. U.S. PMI is illustrative and may end before payoff.

Reference rules: Ontario · Toronto · British Columbia · Federal GST/HST.
